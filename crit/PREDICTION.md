# Pre-crit prediction — written before the review, on purpose

Committed **8 August 2026**, before sending the portfolio out for design review. The point of
timestamping this is that a prediction made afterwards is worthless. When the real feedback arrives it
goes next to this file and I mark each line **hit** or **miss** — including the misses, which are the
interesting ones, because they are the things I still cannot see.

**Proof statement given to the reviewer:** *"I build AI that knows its limits — and ships anyway."*
**One action the site is meant to produce:** an email about an AI/ML role, internship or research
collaboration.
**URL reviewed:** https://farhanbashir.netlify.app/

---

## Question 1 — "In ten seconds, what do I do?"

**Prediction: they will describe my identity, not my capability.** Something close to "you're a CS
student doing an AI internship". I expect nobody to say anything resembling "you build AI systems that
are honest about their limits".

**Why I expect that.** My own content map says the hero carries the claim. It does not. The claim
appears on exactly one page — `plan.html`, an internal process page a visitor has no reason to open.
What the hero actually says is:

```
Hello I'm FARHAN BASHIR
Currently a CS STUDENT / AI INTERN
AI enthusiast · ML engineer in progress
AI Intern @ FlyRank AI — open to work
```

Four lines, all about *what I am*. None about *what I make*. I wrote a claim, decided it was the one
thing my work proves rather than asserts, and then never put it where anyone would read it.

## Question 2 — "Would you believe I'm good at it?"

**Prediction: a qualified yes, with the qualification aimed at the hero rather than the work.** I think
the case studies hold up — they carry real numbers and name their own limits. I think the *hero*
undersells hard enough to make a skimmer stop before reaching them.

**The two words I expect to be flagged:** "enthusiast" and "in progress". *Enthusiast* is what you call
someone with no evidence, and I have a live 11-agent platform, a 10,000-trial simulation, a working MCP
server and an agent loop. *In progress* hedges a claim I could simply make. Both were written months
ago when they were more accurate than they are now.

## Other things I expect to be told

1. **Too many process pages relative to work.** Nine process pages against four case studies. Waleed
   already said the structure confused him; I added one-minute summaries, which makes it legible but
   does not change the ratio.
2. **No screenshots of anything running.** Four case studies, zero images of the actual products.
   Already on the still-ugly list, still not fixed.
3. **"CS student" and the certification count compete for attention.** 17 credentials is a real signal
   but sits in the same visual weight as the work, and credentials are weaker evidence than a live
   demo.
4. **The one action is buried.** "Email me about a role" is the whole point of the site, and the hero
   offers four CTAs of equal weight — See my work, My CV, Book a call, Copy email. Four equal choices
   is the same as no recommendation.

## What I am most likely wrong about

Honestly: the 3D scene. I assume it reads as capability. It may read as decoration that delays the
content, especially on a phone. If a reviewer says the robot is in the way of the message rather than
being the proof of it, that is the finding I would least want and most need — the whole hero is built
around BYTE being the demonstration of the claim.

Second candidate: I may be wrong that the case studies land. I think naming a project's limitations
reads as rigour. A reviewer hiring for confidence might read the same paragraphs as hedging.

## How this gets scored

When the feedback lands, each prediction above gets marked:

- **HIT** — they said it, and I had already seen it
- **MISS** — they said something I did not anticipate, which is the whole reason for asking
- **WRONG** — I predicted a problem they explicitly did not have

A high hit rate is not the goal. If everything is a hit, I picked a reviewer who told me what I already
knew.
