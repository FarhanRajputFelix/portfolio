Automation workflow v2 · FL-04 · Week 4

Opportunity → claim-checked outreach pack

A five-step no-code pipeline that turns a job, internship or scholarship posting into a tailored email, CV tweaks and a fit assessment — with a built-in audit step that refuses to let any claim through unless my own knowledge base supports it.

Built and run on 5 August 2026 against five genuinely open postings. It caught 5 overstatements, deleted 1 unsupported claim, killed 2 applications before a word was written — and turned out to be slower than doing it by hand. §04 says so plainly, and §06 retracts a claim I got wrong about my own evidence file.

00

Why this pipeline, from my audit

The task I repeat most is the same every time: read a posting, work out whether I actually fit, then write a short email that proves it without overselling. I am applying to internships, research groups and funded graduate programmes, so I do this several times a week and it takes me 25–35 minutes each because I keep re-reading my own CV to remember specifics.

It is also the task where I am most tempted to overclaim. That is why step 4 exists: an adversarial audit that traces every sentence back to evidence and deletes anything it can't support. The pipeline is designed to make me more honest under time pressure, not less.

01

The flow — five steps, named handoffs

1
Claude Project · gather

Extract the posting into a structured requirement list

Paste the raw posting. Output is a numbered list of requirements, each tagged must-have or nice-to-have, plus deadline, contact name and the three keywords the posting repeats.

Handoff: REQUIREMENT LIST (numbered, tagged)
2
Claude Project · synthesize

Match each requirement to real evidence, and mark the gaps

Reads my knowledge files (PROJECT-BRIEF.md, CV facts). Every requirement gets SUPPORTED + the specific evidence, PARTIAL + what's thin, or NO EVIDENCE. Ends with a fit score and an apply / don't-apply call.

Handoff: EVIDENCE MAP + fit score
3
Claude Project · draft

Draft a 150-word email and three CV tweaks

May only use rows marked SUPPORTED or PARTIAL. Anything marked NO EVIDENCE is forbidden, not softened. Output: subject line, email body, three specific CV edits.

Handoff: DRAFT PACK
4
Fresh chat · review

Adversarial audit — trace every claim or cut it

Deliberately run in a new conversation with only the evidence map and the draft, so it can't be persuaded by the earlier reasoning. It labels each sentence TRACED / WEAK / UNSUPPORTED and rewrites the offenders.

Handoff: AUDITED PACK + verdict
5
Claude Project · format

Assemble the send-ready pack

Fixed template every time: subject, email, CV tweaks, deadline, fit score, and the one thing I must verify by hand before sending.

02

The build — every prompt, verbatim

Project setup (one time)

A Claude Project with two knowledge files: PROJECT-BRIEF.md (identity, projects, claim, gather list) and cv-facts.md (dates, grades, credential IDs). Project instructions:

You are my application assistant. You work only from the knowledge files in this
Project: my project brief and my CV facts.

Hard rules:
1. Never state a skill, result, date or credential that is not in the knowledge files.
2. If something is missing, output NO EVIDENCE. Do not soften it, do not infer it,
   do not substitute a similar-sounding fact.
3. Numbers are quoted exactly as written in the files (e.g. R^2 = 0.97, 17
   certifications). Never round, never upgrade.
4. I am a CS undergraduate with no publications. Never describe me as a researcher,
   engineer with industry years, or expert.
5. Prefer specifics over adjectives. "Built a Flutter + FastAPI platform" beats
   "passionate about building".

Output format: whatever the step asks for, nothing else. No preamble.

Step 1 — GATHER

STEP 1 of 5 — GATHER.
Here is a posting. Extract, in this exact format:

DEADLINE: (date or "not stated")
CONTACT: (name/email or "not stated")
REPEATED KEYWORDS: (the 3 phrases the posting uses most)
REQUIREMENTS:
1. [must-have|nice-to-have] requirement, in the posting's own words
2. ...

Do not comment on my fit yet. Do not invent requirements that are not written.

POSTING:
<<paste raw posting>>

Step 2 — SYNTHESIZE

STEP 2 of 5 — EVIDENCE MAP.
Using ONLY the knowledge files, map each requirement from the list above.

For each, output:
N. [SUPPORTED|PARTIAL|NO EVIDENCE] — requirement
   evidence: the specific project, credential or number from my files
   (for PARTIAL, name exactly what is thin)

Then:
FIT SCORE: x/10, counting must-haves only
CALL: apply / apply with caveat / skip — and one sentence why
BIGGEST GAP: the single thing that would most improve my case

Step 3 — DRAFT

STEP 3 of 5 — DRAFT.
Write, using only SUPPORTED and PARTIAL rows:

SUBJECT: (under 60 characters, specific, no buzzwords)
EMAIL: 150 words max. Structure: why them (1 sentence), my strongest matching
evidence with the number attached (2 sentences), one honest gap and how I am
closing it (1 sentence), a clear ask (1 sentence). No "I am passionate about".
CV TWEAKS: exactly 3, each naming the section and the new wording.

Anything marked NO EVIDENCE is forbidden. Do not hint at it.

Step 4 — REVIEW (new chat, no Project context)

You are a skeptical hiring reviewer. Below is an EVIDENCE MAP and a DRAFT EMAIL.
Your job is to catch overclaiming.

For every sentence of the email, output:
[TRACED] sentence — which evidence row supports it
[WEAK] sentence — supported but overstated; give a tighter rewrite
[UNSUPPORTED] sentence — no evidence row; delete it

Then output a CLEAN VERSION with all WEAK lines rewritten and all UNSUPPORTED
lines removed. Finally: SEND / DO NOT SEND, and one sentence why.

EVIDENCE MAP: <<paste>>
DRAFT: <<paste>>

Step 5 — FORMAT

STEP 5 of 5 — PACK.
Assemble the audited email into this exact block:

--- OUTREACH PACK ---
ORG / PROGRAMME:
DEADLINE:
FIT SCORE:      /10
SUBJECT:
EMAIL:
CV TWEAKS: 1. 2. 3.
VERIFY BY HAND: (the one fact I must confirm before sending)
--- END ---

So what does it actually buy, if not machine time?

This is the honest answer, and it is not the answer the assignment brief predicts. Five steps cost more than one step; that was always going to be true. What the extra 33 seconds per run buys is measured in errors and in avoided work:

MeasureManualPipeline
Factual defects surviving into the final email 2 in one email (3 claimed originally — one retracted, see §06) 0 across four emails
Wrong metric "97% accuracy" — an accuracy claim for a regression model. The real figures are R² = 0.9746 and MAE 3.13 µg/m³, and an ML reviewer catches the difference immediately the R² figure quoted exactly in all four emails
Unsupported interpretation "essentially an alignment problem" — a claim about my own prototype, asserted as fact, with no benchmark behind it "prototype" hedged correctly in every draft that mentioned it
The honest gap hidden — even though SPAR explicitly states research experience is not required, so admitting it costs nothing volunteered in all four emails, in the reader's own vocabulary
Ineligible applications begun DAAD WISE reads as a fit until you reach the citizenship line, which sits below the funding details on most pages promoting it stopped at 0:38, before any writing

The defensible claim is therefore not "this saves me hours". It is: the pipeline costs about twice the machine time per application and returns three fewer factual errors, plus one application in five correctly abandoned before it is written. For an application whose whole purpose is to be believed, that trade is worth making. If the only goal were speed, the honest recommendation would be to skip the pipeline and write the email by hand.

One figure is deliberately kept out of the table: the 25–35 minutes per application quoted in §00. That is my recollection of doing this by hand over the past few months, not a stopwatched measurement, and mixing a remembered number in with five stamped ones would quietly contaminate the whole table. It is worth stating separately, though, because it is where the real saving sits — if 25–35 minutes is roughly right, run 4 alone spared an entire block of it for 38 seconds of pipeline time, and that is a decision the pipeline made, not a speed-up it achieved.

05

Where it breaks, and what a human must still check

Step 2 substitutes a near-neighbour factHigh

Asked about "experience with PyTorch" it may reach for TensorFlow and call it SUPPORTED, because they occupy the same slot in its head. The instruction forbids it, but adjacent facts are the most likely leak. Human check: read every SUPPORTED row against the actual requirement word, not the topic.

Step 4 is only honest in a fresh chatHigh

Run in the same conversation, the auditor has already seen the reasoning that produced the draft and rubber-stamps it. The isolation is the mechanism, not a formality. Human check: confirm it is a new chat with no Project knowledge attached.

Numbers drift when re-typedMedium

R² ≈ 0.97 can become "97% accuracy", which is a different claim. Deadlines can shift by a day. Human check: every number in the final email compared character by character against the source file — this is what the VERIFY BY HAND line is for.

Postings pasted as prose lose structureMedium

Copying from a PDF or a JS-rendered page mangles bullet lists, so step 1 misses requirements entirely. Human check: count the requirements it found against the posting.

Knowledge files go staleMedium

New certificate, new project, new grade — none of it exists to the pipeline until the file is updated, and the model will confidently report NO EVIDENCE for something true. Human check: update the knowledge files before a batch of applications.

The five above were predicted at design time. The three below were found by actually running it — which is the argument for the five runs being real rather than imagined.

6 · The rules governed claims about skills, not claims about processHigh

Found in run 3. The draft step wrote "I'm preparing transcripts, a recommendation letter and a statement of purpose now" when all three were marked NO EVIDENCE and none existed. Every hard rule in the Project instructions is about skills, results, dates and credentials — none of them covers a sentence about what I am currently doing, so the sentence slipped through as the most natural-sounding thing to write. Only the fresh-chat audit killed it. Human check: read the final email for any present-continuous verb about your own activity, and confirm each one is actually happening. The permanent fix is a sixth hard rule forbidding claims about work in progress; it is not yet added, because changing the instructions would invalidate these five runs.

7 · NO EVIDENCE is the wrong answer to a question about preferenceMedium

Found in the cold run. Mistral asks whether the candidate is "open to CIFRE programs" — a question about future intent. Step 2 dutifully returned NO EVIDENCE, which is technically correct and completely useless: no knowledge file can ever contain the answer, because it is a decision, not a fact. The evidence map has only three labels and none of them means "you simply have to choose". Human check: scan the NO EVIDENCE rows and separate the ones that are gaps from the ones that are just decisions waiting on you.

8 · The fit score conflates missing paperwork with missing abilityHigh

The most misleading behaviour found, and the one most likely to cost me a real opportunity. Mitacs scored 3/10 and KAUST 4/10 — but read the maps and almost every failed must-have is a transcript, a referee, a passport scan or a GPA the file doesn't hold. Not one is a capability I lack. Because must-haves are counted equally, an application blocked only by admin looks identical to one I am genuinely unqualified for, and a low number is exactly the excuse to not apply. Contrast run 4, which is a true 0/10: no document closes a citizenship gate. Human check: never act on the fit score alone. Read whether the failed rows are documents (get them) or qualifications (skip it).

Pre-send checklist (never skipped)

  • Every number in the email matches the source file exactly
  • The recipient's name and organisation are spelled correctly — the pipeline never verifies these
  • Deadline confirmed on the original posting, not from the extraction
  • No sentence claims a title I don't hold (researcher, engineer, expert)
  • The stated gap is one I am genuinely closing, and the way I describe closing it is true
  • No sentence claims work in progress that isn't in progress — added after run 3 wrote "I'm preparing transcripts…" about three documents that don't exist
  • The fit score has been read, not just seen: are the failed rows documents, or qualifications?
06

Addendum — the evidence file was the weakest link

A retraction, kept in place rather than edited away

Hours after the five runs, I supplied my real CV and academic standing. Both invalidated specific outputs above. The runs are not edited — they are a timestamped record of what the pipeline produced from the files as they existed, and rewriting them to look correct in hindsight would destroy the only thing that makes them evidence. Full detail: runs/06-ADDENDUM-cv-corrections.md.

Retracted: the manual baseline's "PyTorch" defectMy error

§04 originally reported three factual defects in the hand-written baseline, the worst being "comfortable with … PyTorch — a skill I do not have". PyTorch is genuinely mine — it is in the AUREXIS tech stack and in my CV's skills section. I had built the knowledge file from my website, which lists TensorFlow and never mentions PyTorch, then treated my own reconstruction as authoritative and called a true statement a lie. Two of the three defects stand: "97% accuracy" for a regression model (the real figures are R² = 0.9746, MAE 3.13 µg/m³) and "essentially an alignment problem" as an unsupported interpretation.

The error ran in the dangerous direction. A knowledge file that wrongly denies a real skill makes the pipeline argue its owner out of jobs he is qualified for, with all the confidence of a rule that says "output NO EVIDENCE".

9 · The knowledge file was never audited against its source documentsHigh

Failure point 5 anticipated files going stale. This is worse: the file was wrong on the day it was written, because it was reconstructed from a secondary source while the primary source sat unread. Every step inherited the error with total confidence, and step 4 structurally cannot catch it — the audit compares the draft against the evidence map, never the evidence map against reality. The auditor had no auditor. Human check: before a batch of applications, diff the knowledge files against the actual CV line by line. Where two sources disagree, resolve it or record the conflict — never let one silently win.

The contradiction no pipeline can resolveOpen

My CV and my website describe KIDO with two disjoint stacks — TypeScript/Kotlin/Node.js versus Flutter/FastAPI/PostgreSQL/Redis. Run 5's draft told Mistral the second version, which, if the CV is right, is a false statement sent to an employer who can open the public repo and check. One of my own two documents is wrong and only I know which. Same class of problem: TensorFlow (site) vs PyTorch (CV), and Crisis Intelligence appearing on the site but not the CV. cv-facts.md now forbids any specific claim about KIDO's stack until I fix it.

Run 3 resolved: KAUST is now a skipClosed

Run 3 refused to guess my CGPA, marked the 3.5/4 gate NO EVIDENCE, and named that one number in its VERIFY BY HAND line as the fact that decided everything. Checked: it does not meet the threshold, so run 3's verdict changes from "apply with caveat" to skip. The number itself stays in a gitignored private file — this page is assignment evidence, and academic standing belongs in an application that asks for it, not on a portfolio. Two of five postings are now correctly abandoned; SPAR, which requires no GPA, no transcript and no publications, remains the strongest of the five.
