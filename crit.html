Survive the crit · Week 7

Nobody could tell what I do

I gave a reviewer my proof statement — "I build AI that knows its limits — and ships anyway" — and asked two questions before anything else: in ten seconds, what do I do, and would you believe I'm good at it. The answer to the first was no.

If you read one thing

The reviewer's verdict: "the site has the receipts for your claim, they're just filed under Process instead of stated on the hero." My homepage sold "diligent student"; my deep pages sold "engineer who knows what breaks."

Five must-fixes, all live. The claim is now the headline, the hedging is gone, the proof sits in the hero, the primary button finally performs the one action the site exists for — and a wall of zeroes that could greet a recruiter mid-load is fixed.

01

What I asked, and why in that order

Two questions, deliberately before any detailed look: "In ten seconds on the homepage, what do I do?" and "Would you believe I'm good at it?" Once somebody starts examining a site properly they can no longer give you a ten-second reaction — that first impression is the one thing you cannot get back, and it is the only test of whether a claim actually lands.

I also handed over the proof statement and the one action the site is supposed to produce (an email about a role or research collaboration), so the reviewer could judge the page against its actual job rather than against their taste.

I committed a prediction to git first (crit/PREDICTION.md, commit 5a86659), before sending the link. A prediction made after the feedback is worthless. Scored in §04, including the one I got badly wrong.

02

The feedback, unedited

Q1 · In ten seconds, what do I do?

"Honestly, the first thing I register is 'AI intern, CS student, lots going on' — not 'builds AI that knows its limits and ships anyway.' The hero says 'AI enthusiast · ML engineer in progress' and 'AI Intern @ FlyRank AI — open to work.' That's a job title and a status, not a claim. The tagline you're going for isn't anywhere near the fold. I have to go dig for it (I only found the spirit of it in your Process section — 'shipped rough,' 'still ugly,' the fix log). Ten seconds gets me 'student portfolio,' not your one claim."

Q2 · Would you believe I'm good at it?

"Closer to 'yes, believable' than 'no' — but not because of the hero, because of the evidence buried below it: the honest defect list, the fix log with a real bug, the 'one line I didn't understand' post, the decision-stack writeup. That stuff is genuinely rare and it's what actually proves 'knows its limits' — not a sentence, a paper trail. Problem: it's positioned as page 8 of 12, not the pitch. You're hiding your best asset behind your résumé."

Everything else, unfiltered

"The four zeroes ('0 Projects shipped / 0 Certifications / 0 Research areas / 0 Tools') — I assume that's an unloaded counter animation, but if a recruiter lands mid-load or with JS hiccuping, that's a wall of zeroes as their first impression. High risk for a homepage."

"'AI enthusiast · ML engineer in progress' reads junior and hedgy — the opposite of the confident claim you told me you want."

"CTA is 'See my work / My CV / Book a call' — none of those is 'email me about a role.' You said the site should produce one action (an email), but the primary buttons don't say that anywhere near as directly as the case-study links do."

"17 certifications up top-of-fold-adjacent is a lot of badge-flexing before you've earned the claim. Cert walls read as 'trying to prove I'm qualified,' not 'I ship.'"

"The self-aware writing is the actual differentiator here — better than any AI/ML portfolio I've seen from a student. But it's process documentation, sitting in a submenu, not the hook."

"Bottom line: the site has the receipts for your claim, they're just filed under 'Process' instead of stated on the hero. Right now the homepage sells 'diligent student,' and the deep pages sell 'engineer who knows what breaks.' Flip that."

I replied "noted, thank you" and nothing else until I had sorted it. The temptation to explain that the claim is on the site — on plan.html — was strong, and it would have been the wrong instinct. A claim on a page nobody opens is not on the site.

03

Must-fix

Sorting rule I used: it is a must-fix if it makes the page confusing, breaks something, damages the one action, or stops the proof statement from landing. Everything else waits.

1 · The claim wasn't on the homepageFixed

Why must
The proof statement is the page's entire job, and it lived only on plan.html — an internal content-map page a visitor has no reason to open. My own content map said the hero should carry it. It never did.
Changed
The hero headline is now the claim itself: "I build / AI THAT KNOWS / ITS LIMITS / — and ships anyway." It replaced "Currently a / CS STUDENT / AI INTERN", which said what I am rather than what I make.

2 · "AI enthusiast · ML engineer in progress"Fixed

Why must
Read as "junior and hedgy — the opposite of the confident claim you want". Enthusiast is what you call someone with no evidence; I have a live 11-agent platform, a 10,000-trial simulation and a working MCP server. In progress hedged a claim I can simply make.
Changed
Deleted. The line under the claim is now just "— and ships anyway." Role and status moved to a single quiet badge: "CS undergraduate · AI Intern @ FlyRank AI · open to work". Still honest, no longer the headline.

3 · A wall of zeroesFixed

Why must
Genuinely broken, and I missed it entirely. The markup shipped >0< and JavaScript animated it up on scroll — so any visitor whose JS was slow, blocked, or whose observer hadn't fired read "0 Projects shipped · 0 Certifications". The worst possible first impression, caused by a decoration.
Changed
The HTML now ships the real numbers. The script sets the value to zero only at the moment it knows it can animate it back up, so a broken-JS visitor sees the truth instead of nothing.

4 · No button performed the one actionFixed

Why must
The site exists to produce one action — an email about a role — and offered four buttons of equal weight, none of which said so. Four equal choices is the same as no recommendation.
Changed
The primary button is now "Email me about a role", opening a compose window with the subject pre-filled. See my work, My CV and Book a call are demoted to secondary.

5 · The proof was filed under ProcessFixed

Why must
The strongest sentence in the review: the honesty writing is "better than any AI/ML portfolio I've seen from a student" but was "page 8 of 12, not the pitch." A claim about knowing your limits is worthless as a sentence and convincing as a paper trail — and the paper trail was hidden.
Changed
A Proof, not adjectives row now sits in the hero, directly under the claim, linking the three pages that actually evidence it: everything wrong with this site, a bug two reviewers found before I did, and a workflow I measured as slower. Each headline states a weakness, because that is the claim.

What the misses actually teach

Five hits are worth very little: those were problems I could already see and had chosen not to fix, which is a discipline failure rather than a blindness one.

The two wrongs are the valuable part. I had catalogued my honest process writing as excess — "seven process pages against three case studies is the wrong ratio", in my own words on my own ugly list. The reviewer says it is the single best thing on the site. I was one edit away from deleting my strongest asset for being too plentiful.

And the miss is the third time in two weeks that someone else found something in seconds that I had looked straight at: six orphaned pages, a robot sitting on my own hero text, and now a row of zeroes. The pattern is not carelessness. It is that I read the page I intended instead of the page that renders, and no amount of re-reading fixes that.
