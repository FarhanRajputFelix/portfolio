Still ugly · Week 5 · updated 7 August 2026

What I already know is rough

This site went live before it was finished, on purpose. A private portfolio helps nobody. Below is everything I can already see wrong with it — written by me, before anyone else had to point it out.

Keeping this page public is a small bet: a reviewer who finds a flaw I have already named learns something different about me than one who finds a flaw I was hoping they would miss.

00

What a real person said

I posted the link on LinkedIn on 7 August and asked one question: what confused you? The post reached 608 people. One person answered, and he works in the field I'm applying to — Syed Waleed Hussain, CS'27 at FAST NUCES, ML intern at FlyRank.ai. Verbatim:

"The main thing that confused me was how the information was structured. A bit more clarity and context would make it easier to understand."

Not one word about the 3D scene, the colours, or the projects. The thing that failed was organisation — a reader could not tell what to look at first or why any of it mattered.

Did the work land? Partly, and that is the honest answer. Nobody told me a case study was unconvincing or a number was wrong, which is mildly encouraging. But 608 impressions produced one comment, and that comment says the reader had to work too hard. Content nobody can navigate is content nobody evaluates.

What makes this worth putting on the page rather than in a private note: I had already written this defect down myself, two sections below — "long pages, no summaries… a reviewer with four minutes gets no help finding the part worth reading". I knew, I shipped anyway, and an outside reader found it within an hour. Naming a flaw is not the same as fixing it, and this is the proof.

What changed because of it

  • Every long page now opens with a summary box — the claim, the numbers, and the one thing worth reading if you only have a minute. Starting with the workflow page, which was the worst offender at roughly 1,200 words before you reach a conclusion.
  • The homepage Process section explains itself rather than presenting eight unlabelled cards.
  • Still open: the deeper version of his point is that seven process pages against three case studies is the wrong ratio. Adding summaries makes the structure legible; it does not fix the structure. That is a rewrite, not an edit, and it is honest to say I have not done it yet.
01

Content and evidence

Two claims on my CV that my own repos don't supportHigh

My CV states MAE = 3.13 µg/m³ for the air quality model and says it "served real-time inferences through a modular FastAPI framework". The repository documents neither — only R² ≈ 0.97, executed locally. My CV also lists PyTorch in the AUREXIS stack, while that repo states plainly that it is rule-based, not neural networks.

Either the work exists and I never published it, or the CV drifted. Until I resolve it, the case study claims only what the repo shows. Fixing the CV is the single most important item on this page, because it is the one that could mislead somebody.

Three projects on the homepage still have no case studyMedium

Self-Regulating AI, Crisis Intelligence and the Laravel builds are summary cards only. Crisis Intelligence isn't on my CV at all, and the Laravel projects have no links — I need to decide whether they earn a page or should come off the grid.

No screenshots of anything runningHigh

Three case studies, zero images of the actual products. KIDO has a live demo you can open, which partly covers it, but AUREXIS's web console and the air-quality diagnostics exist only as description. A predicted-vs-actual plot for the AQI model would be the single highest-value image on the site.

No CV to downloadMedium

A Europass CV exists but isn't linked here, and it needs the corrections above first. Every reviewer expects a PDF and there isn't one.

Nothing peer-reviewedKnown

No publications, no preprints. I describe research interests and build research prototypes; that is a different thing and the site should never blur it.

02

The build itself

The homepage duplicates its own CSSMedium

Every process page inlines a near-identical <style> block. The three case studies share css/case.css, which is the right pattern — the older pages predate that decision and were never migrated. Changing a colour today means editing six files.

The homepage didn't link to a single other pageFixed today

Six process pages were live and returning 200, but orphaned. The nav worked fine — every link in it was an #anchor that scrolled down the same page. Out of every href in index.html, exactly zero pointed at another page of mine, so there was no sequence of clicks that reached them. Nothing was broken, which is why it was invisible: a broken link shows a 404, a missing one shows nothing at all. It is fixed now, but it survived the site's entire first week — live since 1 August, found on the 7th — with me opening that page most days. Which says something about how quickly you stop seeing your own front door.

The captures are still JPEG, not WebPMedium

All ten section captures total ~980 KB. I re-encoded the worst offender in Week 7 and got 20% off it, which is a modest win on the heaviest asset on the site. Converting the set to WebP would roughly halve the lot again. Not done — named here rather than left out of the fix log.

I cannot see my own workPattern

Twice in two weeks now. My homepage linked to no other page for six days while I opened it daily. Then the 3D robot sat on top of the hero text on phones — and I had screenshotted it, looked at the screenshot, and not registered the problem. Two strangers named it in seconds.

Familiarity destroys your ability to see what is actually rendered, and re-reading does not fix it. Only outside eyes and actual measurements do. The contrast failure needed the WCAG formula because I genuinely cannot tell 3.79:1 from 4.5:1 by looking at it.

The 3D scene is heavy and unmeasuredMedium

A ~3,200-particle field plus six meshes, with a reduced count under 760px. I have never run Lighthouse on it or tested it on a mid-range Android over a slow connection — which is what a lot of my actual audience in Karachi would use. I do not know my load time. That is the gap.

Accessibility is assumed, not verifiedMedium

prefers-reduced-motion is respected, ARIA labels exist, Esc closes overlays. But I have not run an audit, tested with a screen reader, or checked contrast ratios on the muted grey text — which I suspect is the failure.

BYTE has a 19-entry vocabularyKnown

The agent refuses what it doesn't know instead of inventing — which is the point — but it hits that wall fast. It knows nothing about the three new case studies or the MCP work.

Everything is hand-testedKnown

Every page used to be verified by me opening it, which is how the homepage went six days without linking to any of them. There is now a link checker (tools/check-links.py) that fails if any href is dead, any page is unreachable from the homepage, or the sitemap disagrees with the files on disk. Still no CI to run it automatically, and still no tests for anything else.

03

Writing and design

Long pages, no summariesMedium

The workflow page in particular is very long. There is no TL;DR at the top of anything, and a reviewer with four minutes gets no help finding the part worth reading.

The process pages outweigh the workOverturned

A design reviewer disagreed with me, and I think they are right. I had this filed as a flaw — too much writing about how I work, not enough work. The crit called the honest process writing "the actual differentiator here — better than any AI/ML portfolio I've seen from a student", and said the problem was never the quantity but the position: page 8 of 12 instead of the pitch. I was one edit away from deleting my strongest asset for being too plentiful. Details in the crit. Original entry kept below so the change of mind is visible rather than tidied away.

Brand, images, plan, stack, workflow, MCP, and this page: seven pages about how I work versus three about what I built. That ratio is backwards for someone with no publications — the work should be the loudest thing here.

Placeholder-ish visuals on the work gridKnown

The project cards use coloured gradient covers rather than real imagery, because I don't have screenshots yet. It reads as intentional; it is actually a gap.

04

Two things I decided not to fix

Not every rough edge is a defect, and pretending otherwise makes this list less useful.

Shipping before polish

Deliberate. The brief for this week was to get a real URL working and put it in front of a real person, rather than spend another week refining privately. That is the correct trade and I would make it again.

No framework

Static HTML, CSS and vanilla JS on GitHub Pages — no build step, no backend, no API keys. It means I can explain every line of this site, which is the actual requirement. A React rewrite would look more modern and teach me less.
