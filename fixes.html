Fix log · Open it on your phone · Week 7

Two people found the same bug before I did

I posted the portfolio and asked what confused people. Two reviewers, independently, reported the same mobile layout failure — and I had a screenshot of it on my own phone that I'd looked at without seeing the problem.

If you read one thing

BYTE, the 3D robot, is position:fixed at the middle of the screen. On phones the hero's role text flowed underneath it — "CS STUDENT" and "AI ENTHUSIAST" were legible through the robot.

Also fixed: a contrast ratio of 3.79:1 that fails WCAG AA, tap targets under 44px, and every image loading without reserved space.

01

What they said

"The robotic character has layout issues in mobile view. Functionality is solid overall, though some UI elements need refinement. All core features work as expected."

— Muhammad Abdullah, BSCS student and web developer

"The main thing that confused me was how the information was structured. A bit more clarity and context would make it easier to understand."

— Syed Waleed Hussain, CS'27 at FAST NUCES, ML intern at FlyRank.ai

Two people, two different problems, neither of them the thing I'd have guessed. What makes the first one worth recording is that I had already seen it and not registered it — I'd opened the site on my own phone and screenshotted the hero with the text visibly cutting through the robot. I looked straight past it, because I knew what the page was supposed to look like.

02

What was broken, and what changed

1 · The robot sat on top of the hero text

Broken
.char-stage is position:fixed with translate(50vw, 52vh) — pinned to the centre of the viewport. On phones the hero collapses to one column and the role text flows straight through that spot. Nothing reserved space for the robot, because a fixed-position element is removed from the layout entirely: the text had no way to know it was there.
Fixed
On screens under 900px the robot now sits above the name, and the hero's top padding is derived from the same two values as the robot's position and size: padding-top: calc(var(--char-top) + var(--char-mobile) + 22px).
Why that way
My first instinct was to nudge the robot until it looked right on my phone — which fixes one screen and leaves every other size to chance. Deriving the padding from the same variables means the text starts exactly 22px below the robot's bottom edge at any viewport height. There is no value of screen size that reintroduces the overlap. Verified arithmetically at 360, 390, 412, 430 and 768px wide.

1b · The fix created a new bug, one layer up

Broken
Moving BYTE to the top of the hero worked — the role text no longer runs through him. But his greeting bubble is positioned bottom:100%, meaning "above the robot", and above the robot is now the fixed navigation bar. The bubble slid under the header and the brand text read straight through it.
Fixed
Under 900px the bubble flips to top:100% — below BYTE, into the empty gap before the name — and the CSS tail flips with it so it still points at him.
Why it survived the first pass
I verified the original fix by arithmetic and it was correct: text starts 22px below the robot at every width. What arithmetic could not tell me is what the robot had been moved into. Only the phone showed that, which is the entire argument for this assignment existing — "checked on a real phone, not just a resized browser". A resized desktop window would never have shown it either, since BYTE only moves under 900px.

2 · A contrast ratio that fails accessibility standards

Broken
--text-faint: #6a6a76 against the #060608 background is 3.79:1. WCAG AA requires 4.5:1 for normal text. It was used on every label, timestamp and meta line on the site.
Fixed
#82828d — 5.33:1. Passes with margin, and is still visibly quieter than the secondary text at 7.54:1, so the hierarchy survives.
How I found it
Computed it, rather than looked at it. I wrote the WCAG relative-luminance formula out and ran every colour in the palette through it. "Looks a bit dim" is not a measurement, and I could not have eyeballed the difference between 3.79 and 4.5.

3 · Tap targets smaller than a fingertip

Broken
The nav toggle, BYTE's tool row, the side-rail social icons and the case-study links had no minimum size — several rendered under 44px, the smallest reliably tappable area.
Fixed
min-height:44px on all of them inside a @media (pointer:coarse) block, so it applies to touch devices only and doesn't bloat the desktop layout.

4 · Every image shoved the page around as it loaded

Broken
No width/height on any image. The browser can't reserve space for something whose size it doesn't know, so each one appearing pushed everything below it down — worst on a phone on a slow connection, which is exactly my own audience in Karachi.
Fixed
Real pixel dimensions written onto all 18 images, read from the files rather than guessed, plus loading="lazy" and decoding="async" on the ones that lacked them.

5 · One oversized image

Broken
10-identity-kit.jpg — 1200×2600, 270 KB — for a screenshot that never renders wider than a few hundred pixels.
Fixed
Re-encoded at quality 78, progressive: 217 KB, 20% smaller with no visible difference at display size.
Honest note
20% is a modest win and it is still the heaviest asset on the site. Converting the captures to WebP would roughly halve the lot again. Not done — it is on the still-ugly list rather than quietly omitted from this log.

04

The lesson worth keeping

I had the evidence and could not see it. The screenshot on my phone showed the text cutting through the robot. I'd looked at it, and my brain supplied the layout I intended instead of the one that was there. Two strangers glanced at the same page and named it in seconds.

This is the second time in two weeks the same thing has happened. My homepage linked to no other page for six days while I opened it daily. Then this. The pattern isn't carelessness — it's that familiarity destroys your ability to see your own work, and no amount of re-reading fixes it. Only two things do: someone else's eyes, and a measurement. The contrast bug needed the formula because I genuinely cannot tell 3.79 from 4.5 by looking.
